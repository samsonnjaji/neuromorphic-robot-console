# NRC Social Impact and Business Model

## Long-term purpose

Improve the evidence available to teams building autonomous systems that must operate reliably outside ideal laboratory conditions. NRC should help researchers identify failure modes before deployment and make advanced robotics experimentation more accessible to universities and smaller engineering teams.

## Potential social impact

- **Road safety:** expose perception weaknesses involving pedestrians, cyclists and obstacles in poor visibility.
- **Disaster response:** support evaluation of robots intended for smoke, dust, rain, darkness and hazardous locations.
- **Agriculture:** evaluate outdoor autonomy under variable lighting, weather and terrain.
- **Education:** provide a shared, repeatable environment for robotics, AI and neuromorphic engineering courses.
- **Local innovation:** make rigorous testing workflows more accessible to African universities, startups and applied research groups.

These are intended impact areas, not achieved outcomes. NRC must validate its contribution through pilots and measured results.

## Commercial value

NRC can reduce engineering costs when it helps teams reproduce failures, compare models consistently, avoid repeated manual testing and document deployment limitations.

## Initial customer segments

1. University and independent research laboratories.
2. Robotics and autonomous-systems startups.
3. Event-camera and neuromorphic hardware/model developers.
4. ADAS and autonomous mobility research teams.
5. UAV, agricultural and disaster-response robotics teams.
6. Industrial robotics integrators.

## Revenue hypotheses

- Research and university licences.
- Developer/startup subscriptions.
- Enterprise deployment and integration contracts.
- Paid adverse-condition validation campaigns.
- Benchmark reports, training and technical support.

Pricing should be based on pilot interviews and willingness-to-pay evidence, not guessed before the product has validated value.

## Impact and business metrics

| Dimension | Metric |
|---|---|
| Safety research | Failure modes discovered before field deployment |
| Research productivity | Time to reproduce an experiment or failure |
| Test coverage | Number of validated environmental scenarios |
| Model quality | Precision, recall, mAP and tracking continuity where ground truth exists |
| Edge efficiency | Latency, memory, power and energy per inference |
| Operational value | Time to diagnose incidents and repeated test effort |
| Education | Active labs, student users and completed experiments |
| Commercial validation | Pilots, active teams, conversion and renewal |

## First 12-month validation path

1. Deliver a reproducible simulator and experiment workflow.
2. Recruit two or three university/research pilot teams.
3. Integrate at least one real sensor recording pipeline.
4. Run a benchmark using labelled adverse-condition data.
5. Compare one conventional detector with one SNN-based approach under identical conditions.
6. Publish a transparent technical report, including limitations and failed tests.
7. Convert pilot feedback into a paid research or startup plan.

## Operating principle

KotlinConf is an accelerator, not the business model. NRC continues after the competition if the research and customer evidence support it.
